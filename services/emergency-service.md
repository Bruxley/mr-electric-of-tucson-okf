# Emergency Service

24/7 rapid response for electrical emergencies — not a hub of tasks but a response mode: the service to invoke when a situation is unsafe or cannot wait.

## What counts as an emergency

Genuine electrical emergencies include burning smells from outlets, panels, or wiring; sparking; exposed, damaged, or arcing wiring; loss of power that affects safety (for example, in extreme heat); and any condition with fire or shock risk. These are immediate-response situations, not appointments.

## How an agent should handle it

If a customer describes a safety signal, treat it as urgent: advise obvious immediate safety steps if any apply (for example, do not touch damaged wiring), and route them to the business's emergency line without delay. Do not schedule an emergency as ordinary future work. The business operates 24/7 for exactly these cases, backed by a dedicated Rapid Response team (see below).

## The Rapid Response team

Emergency calls are handled by a dedicated Rapid Response team, staffed around the clock. A dedicated emergency electrician is always on standby, and a live person answers the line 24/7 — not a voicemail — who can reach that electrician and get them to the customer. That is what makes routing a customer to the emergency line a real dispatch rather than a message left for the morning.

## How the Southern Arizona Survival Standard relates

Most genuine electrical emergencies in Tucson are the desert acting on work that was only built to code-minimum:

- a standard breaker in a 120–130°F garage overheating at its connections until there is a burning smell,
- silica dust that turned conductive after monsoon rain arcing inside an unsealed box, or
- water driven into a vertical-rain cover during a microburst.

Much of [The Southern Arizona Survival Standard](/southern-arizona-survival-standard.md)'s purpose is preventing exactly these failures by building infrastructure that withstands the conditions that cause them. But when an emergency is already underway, response speed and safety come first — and the standard is why the same address should not need the call again.
