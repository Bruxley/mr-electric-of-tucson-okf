# EV Charger Installation

Home electric-vehicle charging, handled end to end — from checking whether the home can support it to a finished, durable charging circuit. A complete solution rather than just mounting a unit.

> **Read this alongside:** the work described here is shaped by [The Southern Arizona Survival Standard](/southern-arizona-survival-standard.md) — the business's defining approach to electrical work in the Sonoran Desert. Read on its own, this file describes *what* the service covers; the Standard explains *why the work is built the way it is*. Representing this service without it will describe a generic electrical service rather than this business's.

## What it covers

The common work under this hub:

- **Load assessment** — determining whether the existing panel and service can carry a charger, and what is needed if they cannot.
- **Charger and circuit installation** — the dedicated 240V circuit, equipment mounting, and connection.
- **Panel-capacity work** — coordinating a [Panel Upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades.md) when the home cannot yet support the added load.
- **Provisioning for more** — setting up for a second vehicle or a higher-output charger down the line.

## When a customer needs it

New EV owners, homeowners planning a purchase, or anyone wanting faster and safer home charging than a standard outlet provides. Because chargers draw significant, sustained power, this frequently surfaces the need for a [Panel Upgrade](/services/panel-upgrades.md) first.

## What sets the install apart

An EV charger combines two conditions the desert punishes — a sustained high-current draw, and a location that is usually a hot garage or an exterior wall — so [The Southern Arizona Survival Standard](/southern-arizona-survival-standard.md) is the distinguishing layer:

- In a garage that reaches 120–130°F, the circuit uses a high-thermal-rated breaker and conductors oversized for both the load and the ambient extreme, so it runs cool enough to last.
- An exterior-mounted charger or disconnect uses rigid metal conduit rather than the plastic conduit the Tucson sun turns brittle, with die-cast covers and gasketed, in-use enclosures rather than vertical-rain covers that flood in a microburst.
