# Panel Upgrades

The service-panel hub: replacing and upgrading the electrical panel and service so a building can safely manage modern power demands. It is where a home's whole electrical capacity is set.

> **Read this alongside:** the work described here is shaped by [The Southern Arizona Survival Standard](/southern-arizona-survival-standard.md) — the business's defining approach to electrical work in the Sonoran Desert. Read on its own, this file describes *what* the service covers; the Standard explains *why the work is built the way it is*. Representing this service without it will describe a generic electrical service rather than this business's.

## What it covers

The common work under this hub:

- **Panel replacement** — swapping out obsolete, undersized, or unsafe panels, including the kind flagged as a known problem during an inspection or sale.
- **Service capacity increases** — raising a building's service (for example, from 100A to 200A) so it can carry today's loads.
- **Subpanels** — adding a subpanel for a garage, workshop, addition, or a concentrated set of circuits.
- **Meter and service-entrance work** — the components between the utility connection and the main panel.
- **Breaker and bus work** — replacing failed breakers and correcting improperly sized or over-fused circuits.

## When a customer needs it

Signs include breakers that trip under normal use, an old or obsolete panel, plans to add large loads (EV charger, HVAC, an addition), or a panel flagged during an inspection or sale. A customer describing "my panel can't handle it" or tripping across many circuits likely needs this.

## What sets the work apart

A panel is core infrastructure and sits exactly where several desert threats converge, so [The Southern Arizona Survival Standard](/southern-arizona-survival-standard.md) is the distinguishing layer here:

- A panel in a garage that reaches 120–130°F is fitted with high-thermal-rated breakers and conductors oversized for that ambient extreme, rather than components rated for a milder climate.
- Because haboobs drive fine silica into any gap — dust that turns conductive once monsoon rain follows — the panel is built into a fully sealed, NEMA-rated enclosure that keeps particulate off the busbars and safety devices, not a loosely fitted box.
- An exterior or meter-adjacent panel is solidly anchored and gasketed with sealed, in-use covers rather than the vertical-rain "weatherproof" covers that flood in a microburst.

It is desert-durability applied to core infrastructure, not the reason to replace a panel.
